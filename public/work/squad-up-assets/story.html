From the outing to a shared order

Full case study ↓

  1. 01 · The outing

    A group visit can be one stop in a larger outing.

    The team described teen visits as happening before or after other activities, as part of a group outing.

  2. 02 · The coordination problem

    One organizer gathers the order and tracks payment.

    The team compared single-organizer ordering with separate individual orders; each approach creates coordination work in different places.

  3. 03 · One shared order

    Individual choices within one shared order.

    Each person builds their own bag. A member can pay for themselves, or another member can pay for them.

  4. Invite

    04 · The core flow

    1. 01 / 06Invite

      The host invites the squad by QR code or tap.

    2. 02 / 06Join

      A guest joins through the app, mobile web, or an assisted path.

    3. 03 / 06Choose

      Each person builds a personal bag within the shared order.

    4. 04 / 06Pay

      A member can pay for themselves or for someone else.

    5. 05 / 06Readiness

      The squad sees whose order is still being built and who is ready.

    6. 06 / 06Pickup

      The submitted order has a shared confirmation and pickup details.

Squad Up’s design process

01 · Context

Ordering as one stop in a group outing

  • 14–19target age range · teenagers

Teens go to McDonald’s when they are coming from another event or on their way to something else.

Team insight

Group outings make ordering and payment harder to coordinate.

Observations, interviews and a survey

Method Site observations, qualitative interviews and a survey.

03 · Synthesis

Two ways groups order, and what each costs

  • 2ways groups order

One person can consolidate an order but must chase payments. Separate orders clarify payment but make coordination harder.

Team insight

Current offerings leave gaps in collection, payment tracking and timing.

04 · Exploration

Exploring the group-order idea

We explored five concepts across ordering, rewards, play and family; the group-ordering idea (Multi-Cart) became Squad Up.
